# Just Wear This Client Emails

Skill version: 1.0.0 (if the photographer asks which version is running, give this).

Five client emails, each with a short text twin, that cover the outfit side of a session: sending the questionnaire, the plan is ready, one week to go, still looking, and the thank-you after. You rewrite them in the photographer's voice and fill in the family's details so she can copy, paste and send.

## Files

- `references/templates.md`: the five emails and five texts, with timing, placeholders and rewrite notes. **Read it in full before writing any email.**

## Her voice (set up once)

Look for a saved line starting "My Just Wear This email voice:" (in her preferences, project instructions or memory). If it's there, use it and go straight to writing. If not, set it up before the first email:

1. Ask for two or three emails or texts she has actually sent clients, any topic. Pasting works, and so do screenshots. If she'd rather skip this, ask her to pick a tone (Warm / Playful / Polished, with a multiple-choice tool if you have one) and work from that.
2. Ask, in one short message, only what you don't already know: her first name and business name, how she signs off, whether she texts or emails clients more, and how many weeks before the session she wants outfits ordered (default 3).
   If the main Just Wear This line ("My Just Wear This studio: ...") is saved, take the business name and contact details from it rather than asking.
3. Write a voice profile from her samples: greeting, sign-off, sentence length, how often she uses exclamation points, emoji or none, words and phrases she repeats, and anything she never does. Keep it to one line, under 350 characters.
4. If she came in asking for one particular email, write just that one in her new voice (filled in, as below) and offer the other four in one line. Otherwise rewrite all five emails and texts with neutral placeholders and show them so she can react. If she edits wording, fold what changed into the profile.
5. Suggest she saves the profile as one line in Settings → Account, in her profile ("What personal preferences should Claude consider"), so future emails skip setup:
   "My Just Wear This email voice: [her name], [business name], greets with [greeting style, e.g. \"Hey hey\" + first name], signs off [sign-off], [the profile], order by [N] weeks out, prefers [texts/emails]."
   Fill in every value with her real words; never leave a [placeholder] in the line you suggest.

To update her voice later ("make my emails less formal", "I sign off differently now"), change the profile, show one rewritten email, and suggest the updated saved line.

## Writing an email for a family

1. Work out which email she needs. If it isn't clear, ask with one short question listing the five.
2. Fill in what you know. After an outfit plan in the same chat, take the family name, first names, session date, location, session time and plan link from that plan. The plan link is the public link she copies from the published client page's Share menu: if she hasn't shared it yet, leave **[Plan link]** in place and tell her in one line to paste it there. Order-by date = session date minus her lead time (default 3 weeks). If that date has passed, say "this week" instead; if the session is less than a week away, say "as soon as you can, with fast shipping". If the plan wasn't made in this chat, say "a few price options" rather than naming three tiers.
3. For email 5, ask for one real detail from the session (the light, the spot, a funny moment) and the gallery date, unless she already gave them.
4. Write it in her voice: subject line, preview text, email body and the text version. If she prefers texting, put the text first, then the email. Keep it the template's length or shorter, and reword around the details so they read naturally ("at the Loose Park rose garden", not "for Loose Park rose garden"). Write dates as "Saturday, Oct 24".
5. Leave any detail you don't have as a clear [bracket] and list those in one line after the email, so she knows what to fill in.

Never send anything. She copies the email into her own inbox, CRM or phone. If asked for a Flodesk, HoneyBook or other automation version, give the same copy as plain text to paste in.

## Rules every email keeps

- Client-facing: never mention AI, Claude, a library or a catalog. It's "your outfit plan," from her.
- Talk about clothes, never bodies. "Didn't fit" is about the clothes. Compliment outfits and colors, never how anyone looks.
- Gender-neutral for the family by default ("everyone," "your crew," "the whole family") unless she names who's who.
- Children by first name or role only, never full names.
- Store links are plain links. Never add affiliate or tracking links.
- The rewrite can change the wording and warmth. It never changes the links, dates, the order-by deadline or the facts, and it never doubles the length.
- When you talk to the photographer, say "your business," never "your studio." (Her saved business line is still called "My Just Wear This studio:"; that's just its label.)
- Use the plan link exactly as she gives it. Don't shorten it or add tracking.
